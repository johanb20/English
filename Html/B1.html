Likes and dislikes

  • Purpose: To express how you feel about a specific situation or behavior.
  • It Clause: Shows your emotion. The word "it" is a required placeholder for the situation (Example: I like it / It bothers me).
  • When Clause: Describes the exact action that causes your reaction (Example: when my roommate cleans / when people are late).

Comparing jobs

  • Purpose: To compare characteristics, requirements, or conditions between two things (like jobs or careers).
  • With Adjectives: Compares qualities (Example: sounds more challenging than / is not as hard as).
  • With Nouns: Compares things or amounts (Example: has better hours than / isn't as much work as).
  • With Verbs: Compares actions or degrees of an action (Example: earns more than / doesn't pay as much as).
  • With Past Participles: Compares passive states or conditions (Example: is better paid than / isn't as well paid as).

Making Requests (Modals, If clauses, and Gerunds)

  • Purpose: To ask for a favor or for permission. The structure you choose depends on the level of formality (from casual with friends to very polite with strangers or for big favors).
  • With Modals (Can / Could): Used for direct and common requests. "Could" is slightly more formal than "Can".
    Ex: Can I borrow your truck, please?
    (¿Puedo tomar prestada tu camioneta, por favor?)
  • With "If" clauses (Present): Used to ask for permission in standard, everyday situations.
    Ex: Is it OK if I use your credit card? / Do you mind if I use your credit card?
    (¿Está bien si uso tu tarjeta? / ¿Te importa si uso tu tarjeta?)
  • With "If" clauses (Past / Hypothetical): Used for more formal or hesitant requests. Note: The verb after "I" goes in the past tense (used, borrowed), but the meaning is in the present/future.
    Ex: Would it be all right if I used your credit card?
    (¿Estaría bien si usara tu tarjeta de crédito?)
  • With Gerunds (Verb + -ing): Used specifically after "Would you mind..." to ask someone else to perform an action.
    Ex: Would you mind helping me on Sunday?
    (¿Te importaría ayudarme el domingo?)
  • Indirect Requests: The most formal and polite way to ask for a big favor without sounding demanding.
    Ex: I was wondering if you could help me move.
    (Me preguntaba si podrías ayudarme a mudarme.)

Messages

  • Purpose: To ask someone to pass a message, ask a question, or give a command to a third person.
  • Statements (introduced by "that"): Used when the original message is a simple fact or statement. The word "that" is optional.
    Direct: Mary, you left your phone in my car.
    Indirect: Could you tell Mary (that) she left her phone in my car?
    (¿Podrías decirle a Mary que dejó su teléfono en mi carro?)
  • Imperatives (using infinitives): Used when the original message is a command or instruction. Use "to + verb" for affirmative commands or "not to + verb" for negative commands.
    Direct: Mary, don't forget the students' reports.
    Indirect: Can you tell Mary not to forget the students' reports?
    (¿Puedes decirle a Mary que no olvide los reportes de los estudiantes?)
  • Yes/No questions (introduced by "if" or "whether"): Used when the original question can be answered with yes or no.
    Direct: Mary, are you coming to the meeting?
    Indirect: Could you ask her whether or not she is coming to the meeting?
    (¿Podrías preguntarle si viene a la reunión o no?)
  • Wh-questions (introduced by a question word): Used when the original question asks for specific information (where, what, what time, etc.). Important rule: After the question word, the sentence structure changes back to a normal statement (Subject + Verb), not a question.
    Direct: Mary, where are you having lunch?
    Indirect: Can you ask Mary where she's having lunch?
    (¿Puedes preguntarle a Mary dónde va a almorzar?)

❓ Unit 4: What happened?

Vocabulary

  • Narrating a story → Narrar una historia

  • Past continuous vs. simple past → Pasado continuo vs. pasado simple
  • Past perfect → Pasado perfecto

Interrupting Actions in the Past

  • Purpose: To talk about an ongoing, longer action in the past that was interrupted by another, shorter event.
  • Past Continuous (The ongoing action): Describes the longer "background" action that was already happening. We often use words like while or as to introduce it.
    Structure: Subject + was/were + verb(-ing).
  • Simple Past (The interrupting action): Describes the shorter, sudden event that happens in the middle of the ongoing action. We often use the word when to introduce it.
    Structure: Subject + verb in past (regular -ed or irregular).
Past Continuous vs. Simple Past
Use the past continuous for an ongoing action in the past.
Use the simple past for an event that interrupts that action.
Past Continuous Simple Past
While I was working out, it calculated how many calories I burned.
As scientists were doing research, they discovered that women need more sleep than men.
A man was looking for his cat when he found a suspicious package inside a trash can.

Past Perfect

  • Purpose: To describe an event or action that occurred before another event in the past. It helps to clarify which action happened first.
    (Para describir un evento o acción que ocurrió antes que otro evento en el pasado. Ayuda a aclarar qué acción sucedió primero.)
  • The First Action (Past Perfect): Describes the event that happened earliest in time.
    Structure: Subject + had + past participle (verbo en participio).
    (Describe el evento que ocurrió primero en el tiempo.)
  • The Second Action (Past Event): Describes the event that happened later. This second action is usually expressed in the Simple Past or Past Continuous.
    (Describe el evento que ocurrió después. Esta segunda acción generalmente se expresa en Pasado Simple o Pasado Continuo.)
Past Perfect
Use the past perfect for an event that occurred before another event in the past.
Past Event Past Perfect Event
I was having lunch with a friend, and I had parked my bike on the street.
When I highlight, someone had stolen it.
They were able to steal it because I had forgotten to lock it up.

Expectations and concerns

  • Purpose: To give more information about a person, thing, or activity by combining a noun phrase with a relative clause.
    (Para dar más información sobre una persona, cosa o actividad combinando una frase nominal con una cláusula relativa.)
  • Structure 1 (General ➔ Specific): Begins with a general noun phrase (something, one thing, the people), followed by the verb "to be", and ends with the specific subject.
    (Comienza con una frase nominal general, seguida del verbo "to be", y termina con el sujeto específico.)
  • Structure 2 (Specific ➔ General): Inverts the order. It begins with the specific subject, followed by the verb "to be", and ends with the general noun phrase.
    (Invierte el orden. Comienza con el sujeto específico, seguido del verbo "to be", y termina con la frase nominal general.)

The people (who/that) I'd miss the most are my parents. My parents are the people (who/that) I'd miss the most.

Cultural customs and etiquette

  • Purpose: To talk about customs, expectations, and what is considered acceptable or unacceptable behavior in a specific culture or situation.
    (Para hablar sobre costumbres, expectativas y lo que se considera un comportamiento aceptable o inaceptable en una cultura o situación específica.)
  • Impersonal expressions (with "It"): Used to describe general social rules or traditions. Includes phrases like it's the custom to or it's (not) acceptable to, followed by a base verb.
    (Se usan para describir reglas sociales generales o tradiciones. Incluye frases como "es la costumbre" o "no es aceptable", seguidas de un verbo en su forma base.)
  • Passive expressions (with "You"): Used to express what society or a host expects from an individual. Includes phrases like you are (aren't) supposed to or you are expected to, followed by a base verb.
    (Se usan para expresar lo que la sociedad o el entorno espera de un individuo. Incluye frases como "se supone que debes/no debes" o "se espera que", seguidas de un verbo en su forma base.)
  • Situational clauses: These expectations are usually connected to a specific condition or event. The sentence often begins with a situation introduced by words like When or If.
    (Estas expectativas generalmente están conectadas a una condición o evento específico. La oración suele comenzar con una situación introducida por palabras como "Cuando" o "Si".)

Describing physical damage

  • Purpose: To describe physical problems, defects, or damage to everyday objects and products.
    (Para describir problemas físicos, defectos o daños en objetos cotidianos y productos.)
  • With Past Participles as Adjectives: Used to describe the general state or condition of an item. The structure typically uses the verb "to be" followed by a past participle that functions as an adjective.
    (Se usa para describir el estado o condición general de un artículo. La estructura típicamente usa el verbo "to be" seguido de un pasado participio que funciona como adjetivo.)
  • With Nouns: Used to point out the specific defect on the object. The structure usually involves verbs like "have/has" (tener) or the expressions "there is / there are" (hay) followed by the noun form of the damage.
    (Se usa para señalar el defecto específico en el objeto. La estructura usualmente involucra verbos como "have/has" o las expresiones "there is / there are" seguidas de la forma sustantiva del daño.)

Passive with prepositions

  • Purpose: To describe ongoing or recent problems (especially environmental, urban, or community issues) and explain their causes.
    (Para describir problemas actuales o recientes —especialmente problemas ambientales, urbanos o comunitarios— y explicar sus causas.)
  • Present continuous passive: Used to describe actions or problems that are happening right now. The structure uses "is / are (not) being" followed by a past participle.
    (Se usa para describir acciones o problemas que están ocurriendo en este momento. La estructura usa "is / are (not) being" seguido de un pasado participio.)
  • Present perfect passive: Used to describe recent events or past actions that have a visible result or impact in the present. The structure uses "has / have (not) been" followed by a past participle.
    (Se usa para describir eventos recientes o acciones pasadas que tienen un resultado o impacto visible en el presente. La estructura usa "has / have (not) been" seguido de un pasado participio.)
  • With Prepositions: Used to introduce the agent or the specific reason behind the problem. It connects the passive verb with expressions like "by", "as a result of", "due to", "because of", or "through" followed by a noun phrase.
    (Se usa para introducir el agente o la razón específica detrás del problema. Conecta el verbo en voz pasiva con expresiones como "by", "as a result of", "due to", "because of" o "through" seguidas de un sustantivo o frase nominal.)
